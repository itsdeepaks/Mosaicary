# Landingfolio Product Study

Status: **research record**  
Verified: **2026-07-31**  
Purpose: identify transferable product and agent-workflow patterns without copying or redistributing Landingfolio content.

## 2. Product surface

Landingfolio is a connected suite rather than a single gallery.

### 2.1 Site and landing-page inspiration

Observed discovery dimensions include:

- text search;
- page type, including landing page, pricing, about, login, signup, and 404;
- industry;
- desktop/mobile device;
- broad colour families;
- exact hexadecimal colour input.

The home experience is optimized for rapid visual browsing with visible category counts, dense previews, and source-detail routes.

This is the most important transferable idea: users and agents often need a specific page section, not a complete-site reference.

### 2.3 Production component library

Landingfolio distinguishes inspiration examples from components intended for implementation.

Observed technology groupings include:

- Tailwind;
- Webflow;
- Figma;
- React and Vue references in broader product copy.

Items may be free or locked and may expose preview, popularity/activity, and source/code access depending on membership.

### 2.4 Template directory

The directory includes third-party free and paid templates and separates them from templates included in Landingfolio membership.

Observed framework/tool filters include:

- HTML;
- React;
- Vue;
- Angular;
- Tailwind CSS;
- Bootstrap;
- Webflow;
- WordPress;
- Figma;
- Sketch;
- Photoshop;
- Adobe XD.

Transferable pattern: compatibility is structured, visible, and navigable rather than hidden in free-text descriptions.

### 2.5 Learning content

The optimization-tip library is organized around concrete page areas and user tasks, including:

- hero clarity;
- benefit-versus-feature framing;
- CTA specificity;
- pricing emphasis;
- social proof;
- sign-in usability;
- form feedback.

Transferable pattern: educational content is linked to the same component taxonomy used for inspiration.

### 2.6 Free tools and adjacent products

Public navigation has included:

- free logos;
- a mockup generator;
- related Figma component and Webflow products.

Transferable pattern: free tools work best when they feed the main workflow rather than existing as unrelated utilities.

### 2.7 Submission flow

Landingfolio offers design submission and presents inclusion as exposure/backlink value. Its pricing page has advertised unlimited submissions as part of membership.

Tessli should not copy a pay-to-submit model because payment could weaken curation independence. Tessli's future workflow should require structured evidence, affiliation disclosure, duplicate checks, moderation, and verification.

## 3. Official MCP

Landingfolio's official MCP page states:

- support examples for Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Windsurf, VS Code, and other MCP clients;
- HTTP transport at `https://mcp.landingfolio.com/mcp`;
- bearer-token authentication;
- a free allowance of 100 requests per token per day;
- a burst cap of 10 requests per minute;
- access to more than 4,600 component examples;
- returned data consisting of section screenshots, category, and a link to the source page;
- tool-call logging for account/rate-limit management;
- no project code, prompts, or files sent to Landingfolio according to its page.

These are provider claims, not guarantees from Tessli. Reverify them before any experiment.

### 3.1 What the MCP proves

- agent distribution can be a first-class product channel;
- section-level visual retrieval is useful to coding agents;
- setup can be reduced to one provider configuration;
- a free quota can attract agent usage.

### 3.2 What the MCP does not appear to provide

The public page does not claim structured output for:

- typography;
- spacing;
- grid;
- density;
- hierarchy;
- motion;
- accessibility;
- implementation complexity;
- similarity/copying risk;
- design rationale;
- licence analysis.

That gap supports Tessli's intelligence-layer direction.

## 4. Commercial model

The pricing page observed on 2026-07-31 advertised:

- thousands of component examples;
- hundreds of prebuilt components;
- templates;
- Webflow, Tailwind, Figma, Vue, React, and related formats;
- commercial usage subject to the licence;
- lifetime, annual, and quarterly payment options.

The product funnel appears to be:

```text
Free visual discovery
    ↓
Section-level research
    ↓
Need to implement quickly
    ↓
Paid components/templates
```

Tessli should not copy this monetization before validating its own research workflow. A better future value layer is cloud research workspaces, team-approved profiles, reference packets, private collections, agent tools, and provider integrations.

## 5. Rights and governance findings

Landingfolio's licensing page permits use of purchased/downloaded items in end projects under its terms, but restricts redistribution of source files/assets and resale as themes/templates or marketplace products.

Therefore Tessli must not:

- ingest Landingfolio's paid components or templates into its catalogue;
- redistribute downloaded source files;
- expose a shared Landingfolio token;
- mirror MCP screenshots;
- cache provider results indefinitely without explicit permission;
- proxy the provider as if the data were Tessli-owned.

The privacy page reviewed on 2026-07-31 contained legacy Headlime references. This is evidence that legal and operational content can drift, not evidence that Landingfolio's whole policy is invalid.

Transferable governance lesson:

- derive counts where possible;
- record verification dates;
- test product/domain names in legal copy;
- treat pricing, quotas, supported browsers, and provider claims as stale-prone;
- require evidence links.

## 6. Product inconsistencies to learn from

Public Landingfolio pages have shown different counts for related inventories, including component-example and prebuilt-component totals. These may represent different subsets, but the distinction is not always obvious.

Tessli should:

- define every count;
- derive public counts from source data;
- avoid manually duplicated numbers;
- label provider claims separately from Tessli-verified facts;
- preserve a verification timestamp.

## 7. Transferable patterns

### Adopt

- separate whole-resource browsing from atomic task/section discovery;
- use structured compatibility filters;
- distinguish inspiration, implementation, learning, and agent interfaces;
- connect educational knowledge to the same taxonomy;
- make the agent integration visible in primary navigation;
- keep setup simple;
- preserve original-source links;
- use free tools that produce inputs for the main workflow.

### Adapt

- replace screenshot-first retrieval with capability-first source selection;
- make external provider results optional and transient;
- add provenance, rights, freshness, and limitation metadata;
- produce a research plan before implementation;
- export a source-backed handoff rather than source assets.

### Reject

- screenshot-volume competition;
- paid/private content mirroring;
- proxying another provider's subscription;
- popularity numbers without definitions;
- pay-to-submit curation;
- copying Landingfolio's visual identity or page composition.

## 8. Tessli opportunities

### Near-term data opportunity

Create structured profiles for high-value resources so Tessli can answer:

- what the source contains;
- which task it supports;
- whether it offers implementation-ready assets;
- which frameworks/tools it supports;
- whether it exposes an API/MCP/CLI;
- what access is required;
- what can be persisted;
- what must remain transient;
- how recently the claims were verified.

### Near-term workflow opportunity

Create a Markdown research packet containing:

- brief;
- selected resources;
- task-specific reasons;
- source links;
- restrictions;
- observations;
- originality checklist;
- implementation checklist.

### Later agent opportunity

Expose native Tessli metadata through a read-only MCP. Treat Landingfolio's official MCP as an optional provider experiment, not as Tessli's underlying data source.

## 9. Landingfolio provider experiment

When Slice 13.6 is approved:

1. obtain a user-owned Landingfolio token;
2. configure the official MCP in an isolated test client;
3. use a fixed set of briefs;
4. record requests, categories, latency, failures, and source URLs;
5. do not log or commit the token;
6. do not commit returned screenshots;
7. compare baseline agent research against provider-assisted research;
8. assess whether returned references improve task fit and originality;
9. document rate-limit and outage behaviour;
10. remove test credentials after the evaluation.

## 10. Conclusion

Landingfolio validates section-level discovery and agent distribution. It does not invalidate Tessli.

The better Tessli opportunity is to understand multiple design sources together, describe their capabilities and restrictions, select the right source for a task, and produce an attributable research handoff that guides original implementation.
